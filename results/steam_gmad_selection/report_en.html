Inputs and selection method

The input contains 342 chunk-score files: 19 original clips × 3 chunks × 6 settings. Each setting combines one of two resolutions (720p or 1080p) with one of three frame rates (30, 60 or 120 fps). Each chunk lasts 1.5 seconds and has 180 proxy scores on a common 120 Hz timeline. Combining all three chunks gives 6³ = 216 candidate settings per original clip and 4,104 candidates in total.

gMAD alternates pooling rules as defender and attacker. Within each original clip, it searches for two candidates that the defender rates nearly equally but the attacker rates as differently as possible. The defender's predictions define 3 quantile bins. Both videos must fall in the same bin; equal predictions are kept together.

  • Defender gap ≤ min(0.1, 5% × defender p95−p5 span).
  • Attacker gap ≥ 10% × attacker p95−p5 span.
  • Among feasible pairs in each bin, select the pair with the largest attacker gap.

There are 19 content groups × 15 defenders × 14 other attackers × 3 bins = 11,970 attack tasks. 11,299 tasks selected a valid pair, yielding 4,410 distinct gMAD pairs after deduplication. A task without a valid pair does not establish that the defender wins.

Pooling rules and score treatment

The 15 rules produce 12 distinct prediction groups on these candidates. The following rules have equivalent predictions for this run:

Equivalent predictions
mean = last_frames_mean = cumulative_quality
median = kth_percentile

The 50th percentile is the median. The 5-second tail window covers each entire 4.5-second sequence; the duration-corrected cumulative-quality calculation also reduces to the overall mean at this duration.

Time policy: converted. The original 25 fps study durations are converted to 120 Hz while preserving their duration in seconds.

The four position-weighting rules use the three chunks: early dominance [1, 0, 0], late dominance [0, 0, 1], increasing weights [1/6, 2/6, 3/6], and decreasing weights [3/6, 2/6, 1/6].

Negative scores are excluded before pooling. This dataset contains 0 negative input scores, so 0 candidate sequences are affected. The input offset is 0; retained scores range from 3.204 to 10.000. Every candidate has 540 scores, corresponding to 4.5 seconds.

Pilot selection

The pilot contains 266 pairs involving 440 distinct videos: 190 gMAD pairs, 38 random pairs and 38 diagnostic pairs. Each original clip contributes 10 gMAD pairs, 2 random pairs and 2 diagnostic pairs.

  • gMAD pairs are chosen greedily to cover additional effective defender–attacker–bin combinations, with normalized attacker gap used to break coverage ties.
  • Random pairs include one pair of fixed-setting videos and one pair where at least one video changes settings. They need not meet the gMAD gap criteria.
  • Diagnostic pairs compare reversed setting orders, or fixed against varying settings with similar mean scores.

The pilot is a subset for an initial study. The full gMAD list contains all distinct pairs selected by successful attack tasks. Video A and Video B are list labels; participant presentation order should be randomized.
